Last year I wrote a zine titled "I want to make a zine". In it, I wonder what changed since I was a teenager, writing into the small hours of the night (school nights, usually), coming up with story ideas left, right and centre (even if they weren't very good) - but mostly about how the boulder of perfectionism now weighs me down, preventing me from finishing even something as simple as an 8-page zine (covers included). The point of the zine was to unblock me, to release the expectations of what my first zine would look like. The theme of not being able to create seemed to be the only topic I could bring myself to talk about.

This post is a spiritual successor to that.

Last summer, in an effort I can only now describe as miraculous, I produced an essay about T4T love that was picked for an anthology - my first piece of writing to ever be traditionally published and the only one that I have ever been paid for. It felt amazing, I still can't believe that it happened, and I can't wait to hold the book in my hands when it comes out next summer.

Yet, since then, I have been unable to create (and I use the term create in the largest possible sense) anything that has brought me anywhere near the same satisfaction. Since then, I have been feeling what can I only describe as creatively constipated.

I know, sounds delightful, but I prefer that term to calling it a block, because it's not a lack of will, a lack of ideas, or a lack of drive. I can physically feel something wanting to come out. But it just doesn't. Projects I should be excited about now feel daunting and above my skill level. Smaller ideas like zines and short stories feel inadequate to the scope of my ambition. And all the while, life has been chaotic and traumatising, making it even harder for me to find the time and emotional energy to tap into my overwhelming feelings.

It's not like I didn't try anyway. I wrote down ideas in my notes app, paid for my first ever novella-length finished draft to undergo developmental edits, tried to implement those edits and got incredibly demoralised about the entire endeavour, organised zine-making sessions with others to get inspired, read books about writing, and even learned basic web development and made this website - cause why the fuck not, procrastination can be productive sometimes. All this in an effort to relieve this pressure. Even creative diarrhoea would be preferable to this. A year later, I am not any better, so I am trying this. The ultimate unblocker. The blank page filler that makes it less daunting to just begin.

Since I can't write about anything, I will write about that.

Instead of thinking about things I want to write, I have been thinking about what might have brought about this constipation. As a teenager, I wrote hundreds of fanfictions and short stories, started many things I didn't finish, and came up with some ideas I still carry with me today. I have been trying to figure out what changed. There are so many reasons I could point to, which probably all contribute to varying degrees, all partially true and partially false, that I thought I'd make a list, in order of how uncomfortable they make me when I think about them.

1. My phone has destroyed my ability to be alone with my thoughts

While it is very likely that the current state of social media has had an impact in my ability to just sit with myself, I have never been particularly good at doing that (ADHD is truly the gift that keeps on giving), and it's not like I wasn't on the internet the entire time I was a teenager anyway. It does take up more of my time than I would like, though, and I believe it has made it way too easy for me to occupy my time and distract my thoughts in ways that do not creatively fulfil me in the slightest.

2. Being an adult means I have too much responsibility and no time left for imagination, let alone writing

This is immediately discredited by the existence of authors who are adults. If anything, being older should be giving me more to be inspired from and write about. That said, there is something about the retrospectively uncomplicatedness of teenagehood that makes it easier to both abstract experiences and believe in your work. Adulthood has a tendency to bog people down with realistic constraints and practicalities that can poison even the purest of ambitions.

3. I don't have a clear end goal for my writing

When all I wrote was fanfiction, the process was clear: I had an idea, I applied it to a ready-made universe and characters I was intimately familiar with, I wrote the story I wanted to write, did a round of edits or two with an equally obsessed writer friend, and then posted it on the fanfiction website we all frequented for others to read. I would get some likes and comments, generating some much needed dopamine, then rinse and repeat. Now, there is no straightforward end goal. I get an idea, I write it down somewhere so I don't forget it, and then don't make it simply because __I don't know where to put it__. I made this website to try and fix that issue, so if you're reading this, maybe it's working.

4. I am still too exhausted and traumatised to create

While life was falling apart and it felt like all I was doing was putting out fires, I "allowed" myself to not be productive, removed the self-imposed burden of creation from my own shoulders and tried to be ok with just consuming. I thought of this as rest, and believed that when I would get to the other side I could finally try and nourish my creativity back to health again. I was so wrong. It wasn't rest if I was actively in crisis mode. It wasn't rest if I was running around like a headless chicken, doing the bare minimum to stay afloat. It wasn't - it could never have been - rest if I felt, and I quote, "like I was being hunted for sport". Creativity cannot flourish in such an environment. What followed from this realisation is that I might still need time, that rest may only actually, finally, possible now, and I need to be patient with myself for a little while longer before I can properly try again.

And then, last but definitely not least, the terrifying, deep-seated fear we've all been waiting for:

5. I just don't care about anything enough anymore

Kurt Vonnegut's first and most important piece of writing advice is to __"find a subject you care about and which you in your heart feel others should care about"__. This is excellent advice, bless that man, and also the one thing that has sown the ultimate seed of self-doubt inside me. When I was a teen, this was easy peasy, hence the scores of fanfiction. I had encyclopaedic knowledge about this little corner of culture that others also intensely cared about, and I used it to write stories others wanted to read in this ready-made universe I knew everything about. So what if the real problem is that now I simply do not care about anything enough anymore?

Of course, that fear is baseless. I care about people, about human rights, about the trans experience, about love and relationships and the bonds that we build with each other, I care about books and weird indie video games and puzzles that feel like they personally hate me, and - most of all - I care about writing, which is itself a hard thing to write about. But picking through all this love I have in search of something to write feels like picking at a plate of delicious treats when you've just been sick with food poisoning. This post is the plain rice I am using to try and get my stomach back to normal.

I am not sure if there is any sort of satisfying conclusion I could write to a post like this. I guess there isn't because the post in itself is meant to be a beginning. I could end it like I did the zine, saying that I will keep trying, and that would be true, I will. I don't think I will ever not write as long as I live. But I hope that having this space to simply post musings and short fiction and maybe poetry, even, will make it so that I can start exercising those muscles again, slowly rebuilding the ability to create that I know is still inside me, somewhere, waiting to make something - waiting to finish anything.

I guess I will leave you with another one of Kurt Vonnegut's rules about writing, and hope that, if you needed it, this post has helped you even half as much as I hope it has helped me.

It simply goes: __"Be not afraid."__
