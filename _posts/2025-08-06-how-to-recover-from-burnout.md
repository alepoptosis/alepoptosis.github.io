Go to bed and wake up at around the same time everyday, and get at least eight hours of good sleep. Invest in a
    memory foam pillow. Invest in a hybrid mattress.
    
Drink at least eight glasses of water, distributed throughout the day. Invest in a good water bottle that will keep
    you motivated to drink regularly. Make sure to keep it clean.
    
Meditate first thing in the morning and last thing before sleeping, for at least half an hour to an hour every day.
    If you have to get up early for work, get up earlier. If you have to take care of your kids before bed, consider it
    was your choice to create an environment that does not prioritise your wellbeing. Invest in a nanny.
    
Avoid using technology for three hours after waking up and three hours before going to sleep. If you have a job that
    requires you to use technology, find a different job that doesn’t. There has to be one somewhere.
    
Eat a large breakfast, good lunch, and light dinner, and take the time to enjoy making all three from scratch. If you
    have a family, eat it with them. If you don’t have the time to cook, invest in meal prep supplies, a home chef, or a
    stay-at-home spouse.
    
Practise yoga for at least two hours a day, in complete silence. If you live in a busy city, invest in a better
    place, ideally outside of town. Invest in an electric car to drive between your new job and your new house.
    
Stay away from social media, TVs, newspapers, radios, graffiti, and billboards. Stay away from the news, your
    neighbours, and the weather forecast. Stay away from the horrors of the world which are both produced by and
    consuming your fellow humans, and close your ears to the tears the Earth is crying for its children day in and day
    out. Reconnect with old friends. Listen to music.
    
Tend to your garden. If you don’t have a garden, that is one more reason to invest in a home that has one. Invest in
    a gardening book or two and learn how to plant seeds and watch them grow into flowers, bushes, fruits, and
    vegetables. Make sure to ignore how the weather doesn’t seem to follow the patterns it used to anymore. Take care of
    your plants and watch them die anyway.
    
Go for long walks around your neighbourhood. Don’t bring headphones, or your phone, or your house keys. Leave it all
    behind and enjoy the fresh air and the sounds of the world around you. Walk slowly, take in the details: the style
    of windows on a building, the sprawling cracks on the pavement, a squirrel climbing a tree, a cloud blowing lazily
    through the sky. Walk without a destination until your feet hurt and the sun has gone down. Walk some more.
    
Find yourself in front of an unfamiliar stretch of woodland, with no recollection of where you are or how long it
    took you to get there. Listen to the quiet of the countryside, interrupted here and there by a long, low bleating,
    even though you’re sure you’ve never seen sheep or goats anywhere. Look at the sky. Is there a new moon? If you do
    not see a new moon, turn back and return home. Only walk into the woodland if a new moon is in the sky.
    
Take off your shoes and feel the grass under your feet. Listen to the sounds of the nocturnal birds, watch for the
    flap of a bat, breathe in the damp smell of moss, touch the dirt that’s gorged itself on dead things since time
    immemorial. Taste the blood in your mouth.
    
Walk for long enough that you’re convinced it should be morning by now. Consider whether you’ve been walking in
    circles. Reach a clearing, sit down, close your eyes and meditate for thirty minutes to an hour. Focus on your
    breath, the rising and falling of your chest, the sound the air makes when it comes into your nose, then out of your
    mouth. Ignore the fact the bleating just got louder. Do not open your eyes.
    
Think of all the things you’ve had to let go. All the love that didn’t work out, all the things you wanted out of
    your life that never happened, all the overwhelming, unbearable sadness that comes from living in a world full of
    people whose lives you cannot, and will not, ever comprehend, yet you are bombarded with every single day. Think
    about the last time you felt your time was truly yours. Think about the first time you realised that all of the
    choices you make do not matter in the grand scheme of things. Think about all the choices you have had to make,
    anyway, since.
    
When you feel a prickling under your fingernails, do not move. Let the feeling crawl through your fingers, up through
    your hand, then in your arm, your shoulder. When you feel it reach your brain, open your eyes. Keep them open and
    watch as It reveals Itself to you. See Its form for what It truly is, and yet remain unable to comprehend It. Feel
    the agony of everyone who has ever and will ever live, then survive after it leaves you. Witness the very fabric of
    the world peeling away around you, revealing that, underneath, there is nothing but the uncaring and just
    consequence of every choice that has ever been made by anyone and anything, however minuscule. Breathe in. Do not
    breathe out.
    
Blink, and It will be gone, but you won’t be. Let yourself fall asleep on the grass. You will wake up in your bed,
    feeling refreshed, energised, and ready to take on the day!
    
Last but not least, invest in good multivitamins.
